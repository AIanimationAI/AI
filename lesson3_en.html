Lesson 3: Animation & Motion

Breathe life into your images. Learn how to animate characters, create smooth transitions and control the camera.

3.1 Finalizing Your Storyboard

Before animating, make sure your storyboard is complete. Double‑check the sequence of events and adjust any shots that don’t flow well. It’s much easier to fix a drawing than a moving clip!

3.2 Bringing Characters to Life

To animate your scenes, you can use AI video tools like Grok or Kling. These models take a static image and create motion – making characters blink, walk or fly【359591517461072†L221-L285】【805035439567289†L154-L160】.

Tips for Natural Movement
  • Keep movements simple: small eye blinks, gentle turns and subtle gestures.
  • Use loops: small repeating motions like a bird flapping create realism.
  • Think about physics: heavier objects move slower and have momentum.

3.3 Transitions & Camera Moves

Good editing makes your story easy to follow. Use transitions to move from one scene to the next:

  • Cut: Switch instantly between scenes to create energy.
  • Dissolve: Blend one scene into another for a gentle change.
  • Pan & Zoom: Move the “camera” left, right or closer to draw attention.
  • Slide: Slide new images over the old to suggest movement across space.
Camera Move Example

Imagine Lucy launching her flying machine. Start with a wide shot of the workshop. Then slowly pan up and follow her into the sky. Zoom in on her determined face as she fights the wind. These moves add drama and keep viewers engaged.

3.4 Upscaling & Enhancements

AI tools can improve image quality before animation. Use upscale options or run your images through programs like Canva’s Magic Resize or CapCut’s enhancers to make them sharp. Remember, perfect quality isn’t everything. Focus on telling your story and adjust visuals only as needed.

3.5 Preparing Clips for Editing

Export your animated scenes as short video clips. Name them in order (e.g., scene01.mp4, scene02.mp4) and keep them in one folder. In the next lesson you’ll add voices and sound effects.

3.6 Assignment

  1. Animate each panel of your storyboard using your chosen tool. Start with simple motions.
  2. Add at least one transition between scenes (cut, dissolve or slide).
  3. Export your clips and organize them for the next step.

Great job! You’re starting to see your story come alive.
