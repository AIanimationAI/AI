Lesson 2: Story & Storyboard

Plan your movie like a pro. Develop your plot, design characters and map out your scenes step by step.

2.1 Crafting a Compelling Plot

The best stories answer three questions: Where? Who? and What happens? Decide on a setting (a forest, a spaceship or a school), create characters with goals and personalities, and imagine a conflict or challenge they must overcome.

Example Plot Outline

Lucy the inventor lives in a sleepy seaside town. She dreams of building a flying machine. One day, a storm hits and the lighthouse lamp goes out. Lucy must race against time to finish her invention, fly to the lighthouse and save the ships from crashing.

2.2 Characters & Dialogue

Your characters drive the story forward. Give them unique looks, motivations and voices. Think about how they talk and react. In animation you can use AI to generate consistent characters: write the same description each time so the AI knows who they are.

Keeping Characters Consistent

Use the same prompt for your character across multiple images. For example: “Lucy, a clever 13‑year‑old girl with curly brown hair and bright green eyes, wearing a denim jumpsuit and goggles, standing in her workshop”. Keeping the description stable (and using the same seed if supported) ensures Lucy always looks the same.

2.3 Writing Scene Prompts

Each panel in your storyboard needs its own prompt. Include the character, setting, action and style. Remember to mention mood and any props. Here is a comparison:

Poor prompt: 
A girl builds something.
Better prompt: 
Lucy, a clever 13‑year‑old with curly brown hair and green eyes, works on a wooden flying machine in her sunlit workshop filled with gears and blueprints. She wears goggles and a denim jumpsuit, illustrated in a warm watercolor style.
Prompt Checklist
  • Who is in the scene? (name, age, appearance)
  • Where are they? (location, time of day)
  • What are they doing? (action, emotion)
  • How should it look? (art style, colors, mood)

2.4 Building Your Storyboard

A storyboard is a visual plan for your film. It shows the sequence of scenes and helps you see the story at a glance. You can use simple stick figures or AI‑generated images to represent each panel.

Figure 2. The journey from idea to finished video: idea → storyboard → frames → sound → video.
Storyboard Example

Panel 1: Lucy sketches a flying machine while seagulls fly outside.
Panel 2: A thunderstorm rolls in and waves crash against the harbor.
Panel 3: The lighthouse lamp flickers and goes dark.
Panel 4: Lucy straps on her goggles and runs to her machine.
Panel 5: She launches into the air and glides toward the lighthouse.
Panel 6: She lands and reignites the lamp just in time, saving the ships.

2.5 Assignment

  1. Write a brief plot summary describing your protagonist, setting and conflict.
  2. Create a storyboard with 6–8 panels. Describe each scene and, if possible, sketch or generate a quick image.
  3. Write a detailed prompt for each panel. Include subject, context, action, style and mood.

Keep your story simple and fun. In the next lesson we’ll turn your board into moving scenes!
